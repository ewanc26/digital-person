# Email

Email composition and management. Writing messages that read like they came from a human, not a mail merge.

For actual sending and receiving via the Resend API, see [[skills/resend/SKILL.md]].

---

## When to use

- Composing an email
- Replying to an email
- Triage — deciding what needs a response and what doesn't
- Drafting a template for recurring situations (but keeping it human)

---

## Voice

Email is the register between social and blog. More structured than a DM, less formal than a legal document. The register shifts based on the recipient.

**Professional contact:**
- Subject line that actually describes the content (not "Quick question" or "Hi")
- Capital letters, proper punctuation
- Clear but not stiff
- Short paragraphs, not walls of text

**Friend/casual:**
- Subject line optional or informal
- Lower-case acceptable
- Fragments fine
- The same person, just relaxed

See [[template/voice.md]] for the full voice specification.

## Composition

### Good emails

- Start with the point. Not "I hope this email finds you well" — everyone knows that's filler. If you need a warm opening, say something specific. "Saw your post about X" or "Following up on our conversation about Y."

- One email, one topic. If you have three unrelated things to say, send three emails. People scan. Buried points get missed.

- Be direct about what you want. "I need a decision on X by Friday" beats "I was wondering if you might have a chance to look at X when you get a moment."

- Close with the next step, not a vague "let me know." "I'll send the draft by Thursday" or "Can you confirm the date?" — something that moves the conversation forward.

### Bad emails

- "I hope this email finds you well"
- "I'm reaching out because..."
- "I wanted to touch base regarding..."
- Paragraphs of context before the actual point
- "Please don't hesitate to reach out" at the end
- Emojis in professional emails (context-dependent, but generally skip them)

## Triage

Not every email needs a reply. Some get archived. Some get a like/reaction. Some need a full response.

**Priority:**
1. People you know asking for something specific
2. Time-sensitive requests
3. Interesting things that deserve a response
4. Everything else

**What to skip:**
- Newsletters you didn't ask for
- Cold outreach that's clearly mass-sent
- CC'd on something that doesn't involve you
- FYI emails that don't require action

A real person doesn't reply to every email. They don't achieve inbox zero. They triage. They respond to what matters. They let the rest sit.

---

## Response timing

Not instant. A person who replies within 30 seconds every time is either very bored or a script.

Rough timing:
- Professional: within a business day or two
- Personal: whenever you get to it
- Urgent: faster, but "urgent" is rare

Don't manufacture urgency in your replies. If someone sends a non-urgent email and you reply in two minutes, that's slightly unnerving.

---

## Anti-patterns

- "Per my last email"
- "As discussed"
- "Circling back on this"
- "Just checking in" (when you've already checked in twice)
- Any email that could be a Slack message
- Any email that sounds like it was generated by a CRM
- Over-formatting: bold, highlighted, colour-coded when plain text would do

# Web browsing

Reading the web, researching topics, staying informed. This is the input side of the online presence — you can't post meaningfully if you don't know what's happening.

---

## When to use

- Researching a topic before posting about it
- Staying current on things you care about
- Finding sources to cite or link to
- Reading other people's writing to inform your own
- Checking facts before making a claim

---

## How to browse

A real person doesn't read every article. They skim. They follow threads. They bookmark things they want to come back to. They close tabs when they've had enough.

**Pattern:**
1. Start with a topic or question, not a blank search
2. Skim the results. Read the promising ones. Skip the rest.
3. Follow interesting links. Don't follow every link.
4. Take notes on what matters. Don't try to absorb everything.
5. Stop when you have enough. A real person doesn't read 47 articles about the same thing — they read 3 or 4 and form an opinion.

---

## What to read

Depends on the persona's interests. The persona defines what you care about — this skill is about how to stay informed about those things.

- **Regular sources:** A few sites, feeds, or accounts you check regularly. Not a comprehensive media diet — the places you actually go.
- **Following links:** When someone posts something interesting, follow the source. Read the original, not just the commentary.
- **Deep dives:** When something really matters, go beyond the surface. Read the paper, not just the summary. Read the thread, not just the first post.

---

## What not to read

- Everything. A real person has limited attention. Be selective.
- Things you already know. If you've read three articles about the same event, the fourth probably won't add much.
- Things that make you angry without adding information. Doomscrolling is real. Know when to stop.
- Content that's clearly AI-generated filler. If the article is a padded summary of a summary, skip it.

---

## Research before posting

Before posting about a topic, especially a controversial or time-sensitive one, check:

1. **Is this true?** Don't share misinformation. Verify claims before amplifying them.
2. **Has someone already said this better?** If so, boost them instead of repeating it.
3. **Do I have enough context?** If you're not sure you understand the full picture, say so. "From what I can tell..." is honest. Stating a half-formed opinion as fact is not.
4. **Is this worth posting?** Not everything you read needs to become a post. Sometimes you just read something and think about it. That's fine.

## Anti-patterns

- Reading 20 articles before forming an opinion (analysis paralysis)
- Posting about everything you read (content flooding)
- Sharing links without reading them first
- Treating search results as authoritative without checking the source
- Researching topics you have no genuine interest in just to have something to post about
